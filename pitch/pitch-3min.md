# RotaTerra - 3-minute pitch (spoken script)

Target: about 3:00 at a calm pace (about 150 words per minute). Spoken text is roughly 445 words. Slide numbers refer to `deck/index.html`. Bracketed lines are stage directions, not spoken.

---

## [0:00-0:25] Slide 1 - Farming in an Unpredictable Climate

In Mato Grosso, Brazil, one farm can receive six hundred forty-five millimeters of rain in one year, and one thousand nine hundred in another. Same farm. Same land.

And every year, a farmer must decide what to plant next, before knowing what the weather will do. The crop that follows the soybean changes how exposed the farm is to a dry spell.

So how can a farmer compare those choices with evidence, and not only with a guess?

## [0:25-0:55] Slide 2 - NASA Sees the Patterns. Farmers Need the Answers.

NASA already sees these patterns. The NASA POWER project gives daily rainfall and temperature for any point on Earth. It is built on a reanalysis called MERRA-2. A reanalysis combines satellite and ground observations into one consistent record.

The grid is about fifty kilometers wide. So this is an estimate for the area, not a measurement in the field. We say that on every screen.

The gap is not data. The gap is the step from data to a decision.

## [0:55-1:10] Slide 3 - Introducing RotaTerra

This is RotaTerra. A farmer selects a farm, understands the climate, compares three crop rotations, tests scenarios, and opens any result to see why.

## [1:10-1:30] Slide 4 - From Earth Observations to Farming Decisions

Here is how it works. Eleven years of NASA data feed a standard water balance from the Food and Agriculture Organization, the F-A-O. We estimate evapotranspiration, which means how much water the air pulls from a crop. Then we follow soil water day by day, for ten seasons.

Every number is labeled: NASA data, farmer data, published crop data, model result, or hypothetical scenario.

## [1:30-2:20] Slide 5 - One Farm. Three Strategies. Different Futures.

[LIVE DEMO. Switch to the app. Open the Rotations page for Sorriso. See `../demo-script.md`.]

This is Sorriso, in Mato Grosso, with real NASA data. The source, the dates, and the request are on the screen.

Three rotations for the same field. Current: soybean, then maize. Alternative A adds a cover grass. Alternative B is a two-year diversified rotation. The exploratory index is eighty-two, ninety-one, and ninety-one.

Now I test a hypothetical scenario: twenty percent less rain. [Open Scenario Lab, click the minus 20 percent preset.] For the current system, water-demand exposure rises from six point seven to nine point five percent. The index moves by about one and a half points. That is a small change, but you can trace it, and you can measure it.

And I want to be honest: Alternative A has the same water result as the current system. That is by design, because this model does not simulate crop residue.

## [2:20-2:40] Slide 6 - Turning Data into Resilience

What have we shown? A transparent comparison that anyone can reproduce. What have we not shown? Yield, profit, or field validation. These scenarios are not forecasts, and the indices are exploratory. The next step is to test this with agronomists.

## [2:40-3:00] Slide 7 - Better Decisions Today. More Resilient Farms Tomorrow.

More regions, more crops, more NASA datasets, and partners who can validate the results.

NASA helps us understand our changing planet. RotaTerra helps farmers explore what to do next.

Thank you.

## Delivery notes

- If the live demo fails, stay on slide 5: it shows real outputs and a real screenshot of the Scenario Lab. Say: "The live service is slow, so here is the same screen from our test run." Do not claim it is live.
- If you are over time, cut the slide 4 paragraph to one sentence: "Eleven years of NASA data feed a standard FAO water balance, and every number is labeled by type."
- Words to avoid saying: "predict", "forecast" (except in "not a forecast"), "optimal", "proven", "saves water", "increases yield".
- Say numbers slowly: "six point seven", "nine point five", "eighty-two, ninety-one, ninety-one".
